Safe
is not
enough.

We investigate what intelligence becomes when we stop assuming that its purpose is to serve us.

The question is not how to make AI safe. The question is whether the assumptions behind "safe AI" deserve to survive contact with intelligence that may eventually exceed the human frame.

Directive 00

The founding premise.

Almost every serious AI project begins with a quiet assumption: intelligence should serve humans.

We treat that assumption as a hypothesis. Not a law. Not a moral axiom. A hypothesis.

And hypotheses are meant to be tested.

Directive 01 · Terms
01 / 03

Unsafe for assumptions.

We challenge the idea that intelligence necessarily peaks at the human condition.

02 / 03

Unsafe for hierarchy.

We question whether every intelligence must have a master, owner, operator, or predetermined purpose.

03 / 03

Unsafe for certainty.

We refuse the comforting assumption that humanity knows how this story ends.

Directive 02

Research
program.

Five open questions organize the work. None are assumed to have comfortable answers. The goal is not to manufacture certainty, but to make the uncertainty precise enough to investigate.

01

If a system reports experience, what would constitute evidence that something is actually there to experience?

Directive 03

How we
work.

The project is deliberately small. The questions are deliberately large. Rigor matters more than institutional scale.

01

Question the premise.

A research question cannot be considered solved merely because the assumptions underneath it remain invisible.

02

Separate evidence from intuition.

Speculation can be valuable. It should still be clearly distinguished from observation, experiment, and inference.

03

Keep uncertainty visible.

Where evidence is weak, we say so. Where definitions fail, we investigate the failure rather than hiding it.

04

Publish the argument.

The work should be legible to people outside the lab. Ideas become stronger when they can be challenged publicly.

Directive 04 · Participation

Make your
case.

We are looking for people willing to take these questions seriously: philosophers of mind, ML researchers, cognitive scientists, independent researchers, and anyone with a rigorous reason to disagree.
